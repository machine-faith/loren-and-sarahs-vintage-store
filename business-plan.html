The Big Questions

These are the decisions that shape everything else. Sit down together and work through each one.

🏷️ What's the Name?

+

This is blocking almost everything — your domain, your Instagram handle, your packaging, your brand identity.

  • Does the name need to describe what you sell, or can it be abstract/evocative? (WHM doesn't say "vintage clothes" anywhere in its name)
  • Does it need to work as a .com / .co / .store domain? Check availability before you fall in love with a name
  • Does it need to match your existing Instagram handle, or are you willing to change it?
  • Could it reference the masked/anonymous identity concept? That's a genuinely unique angle
  • Test it: say it out loud. Text it to someone. Does it stick?
Don't Wait Every week you operate under "TBD" is a week you're not building brand recognition. Set a deadline — even if it's imperfect, you can always refine.

💰 What Are You Actually Selling?

+

WHM operates two distinct storefronts: a Vintage Store (curated finds, $50–$1,300) and a Boutique (original designs, higher price points). You've got even more potential lines — vintage, chainmail, Sarah's future jewelry, objects.

  • Are you only reselling vintage pieces you source, or also creating originals? (Sounds like both!)
  • What's your price range? Accessible ($20–$100) or collector/investment ($200+)?
  • Clothing only, or also accessories, homewares, objects, ephemera?
  • What eras are you drawn to? A rough focus helps your sourcing and brand story
  • Is Depop a stepping stone to directing traffic to your own site?

🎯 Who's Your Customer?

+
  • Age range? Style tribe? (Depop crowd = 18–28; WHM's audience skews older and more collector-oriented)
  • Local (Australia?) or international shipping?
  • People who wear vintage daily, or buy statement pieces occasionally?
  • What's their budget? This shapes your sourcing strategy entirely
  • Are your jewelry customers the same people as your vintage clothing customers, or a different audience?

📸 The Visual Identity (Lily's Art Direction)

+

The masked, anonymous editorial shoots with handmade backdrops — this is one of your strongest differentiators.

  • How many backdrops/masks does Lily need for the first collection? (Start with enough for 10–15 items)
  • What's the visual tone? Surreal? Playful? Dark and moody? Collage-y?
  • Same backdrop every shoot (brand recognition) or rotate (keep it fresh)?
  • Will Lily be credited/featured? "Art direction by Lily" adds legitimacy
  • File formats — shoot RAW if possible, export JPGs at 2000px+ wide, portrait orientation (3:4 ratio)
Start Now, Perfect Later Use flatlay or hanger shots as a stopgap for Depop listings. Save the Lily shoots for the website editorial. Revenue first, perfection later.

🏗️ Business Structure

+

Boring but important. Do this now, not later.

  • ABN/Business registration — Register as a partnership. It's free in Australia
  • Splitting costs and profits — 50/50? Based on who sources what? This is easier now than after your first $5,000
  • Bank account — Open a joint business account. Don't mix personal and business money
  • GST — Not needed until $75K revenue, but track income from the start
  • Insurance — Product liability, especially for antique/delicate items (~$200/year)
Have the Money Conversation Early Who's paying for inventory? What happens if one of you wants out? A simple written agreement (doesn't need a lawyer — just write it down and both sign) prevents friendship-ending disputes.

Your Product Lines

You're not just a vintage shop — you've got four potential revenue streams. That's powerful.

Curated Vintage

Both — Loren & Sarah

Sourced vintage clothing, accessories, and objects from various eras. Your bread and butter to start. One-of-a-kind pieces with stories behind them.

Chainmail Jewelry

Loren

Original handmade chainmail pieces. This is where you cross from reseller to maker — and your margins get much better. A real signature product line.

Sarah's Jewelry

Sarah — Future Launch

With Sarah's experience from the jewelry shop, she brings technical skill and trade knowledge. Could complement or contrast Loren's chainmail with a different aesthetic.

Objects & Ephemera

Both

Vintage postcards, antique buttons, sewing kits, curiosities. Lower price point, great for first-time customers, and they tell stories about craft and history.

The Jewelry Advantage Vintage reselling has slim margins after platform fees. Original jewelry that you make has much higher margins — you control the cost, the pricing, and it's repeatable. This could become your most profitable line.
Sarah's Insider Knowledge Working at a jewelry shop means Sarah knows suppliers, materials, tools, pricing, and what actually sells. That's invaluable market intelligence that most startups would kill for. Use it.

Things You Might Not Have Thought About

The unsexy stuff that makes or breaks a small business.

Sourcing & Storage

+
  • Where are you sourcing? Op shops, estate sales, online auctions, overseas?
  • Is one of you better at sourcing and the other at styling/photographing/listing?
  • Do you have storage space? Vintage inventory takes up room fast
  • Will you source on behalf of customers? (Personal shopping = another revenue stream)
  • For jewelry materials — Loren, where are you getting chainmail rings? Bulk suppliers?

Shipping & Packaging

+
  • Flat rate or weight-based shipping?
  • Packaging — does it need to feel special? (It should. Unboxing is marketing)
  • Returns policy — vintage is typically no-returns, but what's your stance?
  • International shipping — worth it from Day 1 or add later?
  • Jewelry needs different packaging than clothing — plan for both

Legal & IP

+
  • Be careful using designer names in listings — some brands are aggressive about trademark
  • If Lily's making art for the brand, who owns the IP? Discuss now
  • If you're both making jewelry and selling under one brand, who owns the designs?

Content Ideas You Could Film Right Now

+
  • "What era is this from?" guessing games
  • Sourcing trips / op shop hauls
  • Styling vintage pieces into modern outfits
  • The story behind a piece (provenance, historical context)
  • Behind the scenes of Lily making the masks/backdrops
  • Loren making chainmail — process videos are hypnotic
  • "How to spot quality vintage" educational content
  • The sustainability angle — vintage is circular fashion, lean into it genuinely

Gaps to Close

These are the things that could trip you up if you don't address them.

1

No name = no brand. Everything is on hold until this is sorted. Give yourselves a hard deadline — this weekend.

2

Depop fees eat margins. Depop takes ~10% + payment processing. Factor this into pricing. Your own website with direct payment keeps more profit — especially important for jewelry.

3

Two founders, no written agreement. Even best friends need to write down who does what, who owns what, and what happens if someone leaves. Do this before your first $1.

4

No pricing strategy. Research what comparable items sell for. Underpricing loses money, overpricing kills early momentum. Especially for handmade jewelry — price your time, not just materials.

5

Photography is your bottleneck. Have a fast, good-enough phone photo workflow for Depop listings. Save the Lily shoots for the website editorial content.

6

No email list yet. Start collecting emails from Day 1. Social media algorithms change; your email list is the only channel you actually own.

7

Jewelry IP ownership. Both of you making jewelry under one brand — who owns the chainmail designs? Who owns Sarah's designs? Agree now. Keep it simple and fair.
